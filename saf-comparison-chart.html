Where the Americas SAF opportunity actually sits for Singapore

Brazil scores well across every dimension that matters. The U.S. carries the region's largest existing capacity but a narrowed Singapore role. Canada's production case is weak almost everywhere it's scored — except the one dimension that counts most for Singapore, because its value is the cross-border corridor, not the domestic market.

Canada's outlier cell is the point, not an anomaly. Feedstock, policy certainty and project maturity all read Low or Low–Medium — yet near-term Singapore relevance still reads Medium, the same score as the U.S. That gap exists entirely because 45Z's North American-content rule turned Canada into a feedstock corridor, not because Canada is building SAF capacity.

Third-country corridors 45Z re-nationalisation opened up

Argentina→Canada
Soy oil feeding Braya Renewable Fuels: 757,000 tonnes in 2024, up from 48,000 tonnes in 2023. The flagship corridor — best-documented, concrete tonnage trajectory. Braya exports 100% of output to the U.S. Feedstock · trade finance & structuring
Mexico / Canada→U.S.
Tallow and UCO flowing into 45Z-eligible U.S. supply chains — the domestic-content rule gives this material a policy-driven premium over non-North American feedstock. Feedstock · trade finance & structuring
Brazil→U.S. / Europe
Finished SAF, not feedstock: Acelen's Bahia biorefinery has ~90% of volumes pre-contracted, with the U.S. and Europe named as primary export markets. A different kind of flow — Singapore's angle is certificate trade, not physical logistics. Finished fuel · book-and-claim
