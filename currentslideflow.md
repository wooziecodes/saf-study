Intended narrative flow
Set the strategic question. Establish the study objective: understand the Americas SAF landscape and identify how Singapore can capture value as an Asian SAF hub.
Build the common foundation. Introduce biofuels, explain transport decarbonisation, and show why policy, mandates, energy security and demand are driving growth.
Narrow from biofuels to SAF. Explain why aviation is hard to abate, why SAF is the near-term solution, the market outlook, and the relative roles of HEFA, PtL and future cellulosic pathways.
Explain industry economics and structure. Add the missing cost/economics story, then map the SAF value chain and stakeholder roles.
Connect the global market to the Americas and Singapore. Cover U.S./Americas policy, feedstock and SAF trade flows, Singapore's existing strengths, and Singapore's national/sector strategy.
Conduct market deep dives. Assess the U.S., Canada and selected Latin American markets using a common framework: policy, feedstock, production, innovation, players, outlook, strengths/challenges and opportunities for Singapore.
Synthesize into action. Compare markets, form North America and Latin America house views, identify concrete opportunities for Singapore/EnterpriseSG, relevant industry engagement and next steps.
Structural observations
The deck is a working outline rather than a finished narrative. Slides 9, 11, 15 and 17 are explicit placeholders; slides 13–14 have existing content but still carry drafting notes.

The bridge between the global SAF primer and the country deep dives is intended to be: economics → ecosystem/value chain → policy/trade flows → Singapore strengths and strategy.

EU’s ReFuelEU Maritime provides some policy push, but:
Uptake is fragmented and limited.

At a Glance: Europe’s SAF Direction & Challenges
Strong Long-Term Commitment & Investment
Aviation Industry Faces Cost Pressures, but Outlook Improves
HEFA Now, Investing in PtL for the Future
Limited Viability for SAF Trade flows with Asia
European countries are making ambitious SAF mandates and channeling significant investments into both established and emerging SAF technologies.​
This reflects a clear, strategic commitment to decarbonizing aviation and building a sustainable SAF industry for the future.
The high cost and limited supply of SAF, especially in early stages, create adoption resistance within the aviation sector.​
However, as production scales and revenue certainty mechanisms improve, industry acceptance is expected to increase, particularly with enhanced government incentives and support.
In the short term, HEFA-based SAF will remain dominant, despite Europe’s feedstock supply constraints.
Long-term, policy signals and mandates clearly prioritize PtL (power-to-liquid) fuels, leveraging green hydrogen and renewable electricity, setting the stage for a major industry shift.​
Cross-regional SAF trade (e.g. from Singapore to Europe) is not feasible due to lifecycle emissions and logistics costs (according to IATA), highlighting the need for local PtL production and bilateral tech collaboration.

Challenge: ASEAN-origin feedstocks (e.g., palm residues, coconut husks) are often excluded under EU sustainability frameworks, limiting export potential.
