WORKING HYPOTHESIS
The Americas are likely to be one of the most important SAF supply regions through 2030. However, the opportunity for Singapore is shifting. The previous route (Asia-origin used cooking oil (UCO) aggregated through Singapore and sold into the U.S. renewable fuels market) is becoming less attractive as U.S. policy favours North American feedstock. Singapore should therefore focus less on pass-through UCO trade into the U.S., and more on building a role in new SAF corridors linking Latin American feedstock, production projects and offtake demand in Europe and Asia. Brazil should be the priority, with Colombia and Chile monitored for longer-term opportunities.[AW1] 

 
Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) is a substitute for conventional jet fuel made from waste oils, biomass or synthetic pathways. It can reduce lifecycle emissions by roughly 60–80% compared with fossil kerosene, depending on feedstock and production pathway. SAF has become a core policy tool for aviation decarbonisation because airlines have few near-term alternatives for cutting long-haul emissions. In the Americas, the United States has the largest existing production base, Brazil has the clearest feedstock and policy advantages, and Canada, Colombia and Chile are developing more targeted positions in the value chain. The main negative shift for Singapore is in the U.S., where new rules reduce the attractiveness of non-North American feedstock, including Asian UCO flows that Singapore-based trading houses have previously helped move.
This report reviews six Americas markets and assesses where Singapore-based companies can participate credibly over 2026–2030.
Three judgments frame the analysis:
 1. U.S. SAF growth will increasingly rely on North American feedstock. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) restricts the 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit to feedstock grown or produced in the U.S., Canada or Mexico from 2026, and cuts the maximum SAF credit from $1.75 to $1.00 per gallon.
 2. Brazil is the best-positioned Americas market. Acelen/Mubadala's planned $3 billion, 1-billion-litre-per-year Bahia biorefinery, together with Brazil's legislated SAF emissions-reduction pathway to 2037, gives Brazil the strongest mix of feedstock availability, policy certainty and project scale.
 3. Chile, Colombia and Mexico are longer-term options rather than near-term volume markets. Chile offers an e-fuels pathway, Colombia has palm-based and e-SAF potential, and Mexico may become relevant through UCO and nearshoring-linked demand, but none is likely to produce meaningful SAF volumes before 2029–2030.
1. Background & Key Objectives
We are assessing the Sustainable Aviation Fuel opportunity across the Americas to determine where Singapore-based companies (trading houses, feedstock aggregators, technology licensors, investors and the aviation/bunkering ecosystem) can most credibly participate in the region's build-out.
1.1 What Biofuels Are, and Why They Are Growing
Biofuels are fuels derived from biomass or organic waste rather than fossil sources. They include ethanol from sugarcane or corn, biodiesel and renewable diesel from vegetable oils, waste oils and animal fats, biomethane, and Sustainable Aviation Fuel. Demand across the Americas is being driven by three main factors:
Policy mandates and tax credits. Blending mandates, such as Brazil's E30/B15 requirements and Canada's Clean Fuel Regulations, and production tax credits, such as the U.S. 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit, are turning biofuels from a voluntary SAF sustainability purchase into a compliance-driven market with more predictable demand.
Energy security. Governments are also using biofuels to reduce exposure to imported crude and oil-price volatility. Brazil's ethanol and biodiesel programme is the clearest example, and similar concerns are now shaping U.S. and Canadian policy.
Decarbonisation commitments. ICAO's CORSIA scheme becomes mandatory for all member states from 2027, after the voluntary phase from 2021–2026. IATA members have also committed to net-zero aviation emissions by 2050, with expected to deliver roughly 65% of the required emissions reduction.
 
1.2 Introduction to SAF
SAF is a “drop-in” fuel: it is chemically compatible with existing jet engines and airport fuelling infrastructure at blend ratios of up to 50%, and can substitute for part of the fossil content in conventional jet fuel. It matters because aviation has limited near-term alternatives to liquid fuels, especially for long-haul flights. Unlike road transport, commercial aviation cannot rely on battery electrification at scale in the near term.

2. SAF Market Context
2.1 Unit Economics and Pricing
SAF is likely to remain more expensive than fossil jet fuel because feedstocks are limited, production pathways are capital intensive, and demand is still largely policy-driven rather than purely commercial.
EASA's 2024 reference price for HEFA-based SAF was €2,085/tonne against €734/tonne for conventional jet fuel — roughly a 3x premium — while e-SAF/PtL was priced at €7,695/tonne, roughly 10x.
IATA estimates airlines paid a collective USD 2.9 billion premium for the 1.9 million tonnes of SAF available in 2025, of which USD 1.4 billion was the structural price spread over fossil jet.
Global SAF production is forecast at only 2.4 million tonnes in 2026 — about 0.8% of total jet fuel consumption — with IATA warning that growth is slowing just as e-SAF sub-mandates approach in the UK (2028) and EU (2030).
Platts' SAF price assessment (CIF Northwest Europe) has shown roughly 4.7x the volatility of fossil jet fuel since its September 2023 launch (a $444/mt standard deviation versus $95/mt for fossil jet) — a function of a thin, early-stage, oligopolistic market.
Two policy models have emerged for managing this premium: Europe's “polluter pays” approach (ReFuelEU Aviation mandates place the cost on fuel suppliers, who pass it to airlines and passengers) versus the U.S. approach of taxpayer-funded production credits (45Z, formerly 40B) that socialise the premium. Singapore has adopted a third, hybrid model  (a fixed, centrally-procured passenger/cargo levy)
 
2.2 Feedstock and SAF Trade Flows
For Singapore, the key trade-flow issue in 2025–2026 is the overlap between rising global demand for used cooking oil (UCO), U.S.-China tariff escalation, and the new U.S. domestic-content requirement under the 45Z tax credit.
THE U.S. FEEDSTOCK RE-NATIONALISATION
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), signed July 4, 2025, restricts 45Z-eligible feedstock to material “produced or grown” in the United States, Canada or Mexico from 2026 onward. This excludes sugarcane, soybeans, tallow and used oils sourced from outside North America.

 This is already changing trade flows. Chinese UCO exports to the United States fell 55% year-on-year, from 1.2 million tonnes in Jan–Nov 2024 to 540,000 tonnes over the same period in 2025, as tariffs and the coming 45Z restriction pushed more volumes toward Europe.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR SINGAPORE
[AW2] Singapore-based trading houses have historically helped aggregate and finance Asia-origin UCO cargoes bound for U.S. renewable diesel and SAF refiners, From 2026, that route is likely to become much less attractive for U.S.-bound volumes because non-North American feedstock will no longer qualify for 45Z. Asian UCO is therefore more likely to be redirected toward Europe, while U.S. demand shifts toward domestic, Canadian and Mexican material. Singapore will need to adapt from Asia-to-U.S. UCO intermediation toward other roles, including traceability, trade finance, offtake structuring and Latin America-linked supply corridors.

2.4 Singapore's SAF Targets and Strategy
The Civil Aviation Authority of Singapore's (CAAS) Sustainable Air Hub Blueprint, launched in 2024, sets a national SAF uplift target of 1% for all flights departing Singapore from 2026, rising to 3–5% by 2030, subject to global SAF availability.
Financing model: rather than a supplier-side blending mandate (the EU/UK model), Singapore uses a mandatory SAF Levy on passengers, cargo and general/business aviation departing Changi and Seletar from 1 October 2026 (applicable to tickets sold from 1 April 2026), collected into a statutory SAF Fund.
Central procurement: the purpose-built Singapore Sustainable Aviation Fuel Company Ltd. (SAFCo) manages procurement, allocation and administration of SAF and its environmental attributes on behalf of the Fund, and is separately launching a voluntary SAF procurement trial to aggregate corporate demand alongside the regulated levy.
Cost impact: Government estimates put the 2026 levy at roughly S$1–3 for short-haul economy tickets (e.g., Bangkok) rising to about S$6.40–16 for long-haul (e.g., London), depending on the vintage of the estimate; SAF itself is estimated to cost three to four times conventional jet fuel.
Regional platform: CAAS launched the Asia-Pacific Sustainable Aviation Centre (APSAC) in July 2025 to conduct region-specific SAF policy research and explore regional feedstock options. This gives Singapore a platform to engage producers and policymakers on how Americas-origin SAF supply could connect to Asia-Pacific demand.

United States
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
U.S. SAF policy underwent its most significant change in years with the OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025), which restructured the 45Z Clean Fuel Production Credit — the successor to the expired 40B Sustainable Aviation Fuel Credit and biodiesel blenders' credit. Key features:
Extended through December 31, 2029 (from an original 2027 sunset), giving producers multi-year investment certainty.
Maximum SAF credit value cut from $1.75/gallon to $1.00/gallon (a 43% reduction), removing the SAF-specific premium that had existed over non-aviation clean fuels; base statutory rates are around $0.35/gallon for SAF and $0.20/gallon for non-aviation fuel, scaling up to 5x with prevailing-wage-and-apprenticeship (PWA) compliance.
Feedstock restricted to material produced or grown in the U.S., Canada or Mexico (the most consequential change for global feedstock trade)
Indirect land-use change (ILUC) penalties removed from emissions calculations for fuel produced after Dec 31, 2025, and “foreign entity of concern” ownership restrictions added, with Treasury issuing 170 pages of proposed implementing regulations on Feb 3, 2026 (comment period closed April 2026).
The FAA's Fueling Aviation's Sustainable Transition (FAST) grant program — which had awarded $291 million across 22 projects in August 2024 — had its unobligated balances rescinded under OBBBA, shifting the policy burden from grants to tax credits and shifting the centre of gravity to state-level programmes (California and Washington's LCFS-style clean fuel programmes; tax incentives in Illinois, Iowa, Colorado, Arkansas and Minnesota). The original policy ambition remains the Biden-era SAF Grand Challenge target of 3 billion gallons of domestic SAF production by 2030, though the OBBBA's reduced SAF-specific incentive value makes that target considerably harder to reach on current trajectories.
 
 
FEEDSTOCK
The U.S. feedstock base is dominated by used cooking oil, animal tallow, soybean oil and corn/distillers' corn oil, supplemented — pre-2026 — by substantial imported UCO (over 3 billion pounds annually by 2023, with more than half from China). The North American-content restriction in 45Z is expected to redirect demand toward domestic corn, soy and canola oil, and toward Canadian and Mexican tallow and UCO — with U.S. UCO imports overall showing trade diversion (shifting suppliers) rather than a volume collapse through 2025.
 
 
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
U.S. SAF production capacity grew roughly 1,400% from early 2024 through 2025, though from a very low base.
Diamond Green Diesel (Valero / Darling Ingredients JV) — the world's second-largest renewable diesel producer at 1.2 billion gal/yr across Port Arthur, TX and Norco, LA — completed a SAF conversion project at Port Arthur in Q4 2024 giving it nameplate SAF capacity of 235 million gal/yr (up to 50% of that plant's 470 million gal/yr renewable diesel capacity).
World Energy, Paramount, CA — the world's first commercial-scale SAF facility (converted from an existing refinery, producing since 2016), targeting roughly 250 million gal/yr, with customers including United, JetBlue, Amazon Air and Etihad.
Montana Renewables (Calumet), Great Falls, MT — expanding SAF output from ~30 million gal/yr to a targeted 150 million gal/yr in 2026 and 300 million gal/yr by 2028, backed by a $1.44 billion DOE loan guarantee issued in the final months of the Biden administration and subsequently allowed to proceed by the Trump administration.
Phillips 66 (Rodeo Renewed, CA) brought roughly 10,000 barrels/day of SAF capacity online in Q3 2024, though production was temporarily halted in Q4 2024.
Neste has built out U.S. distribution via its Galena Park terminal on the Houston Ship Channel (33.5 million gal of blending/storage capacity) and, via the Martinez Renewables JV with Marathon Petroleum, a California conversion project.
Roughly 110 million gallons of neat SAF were supplied in the U.S. in the most recent full year cited by industry trackers, of which about 70 million gallons were imported — underscoring that even the U.S., with the region's largest capacity base, still runs a SAF deficit against airline demand.
R&D LANDSCAPE
U.S. SAF R&D runs through DOE national labs (Bioenergy Technologies Office, NREL) on advanced pathways (alcohol-to-jet, Fischer-Tropsch, power-to-liquid), the DOE's 45ZCF-GREET lifecycle-emissions model that underpins 45Z eligibility determinations, and a dense web of land-grant university programmes tied to feedstock carbon-intensity science (Iowa State, Illinois, Nebraska) — most immediately relevant through USDA's Feedstock Carbon Intensity Calculator (FD-CIC), still being finalised as of April 2026, which will determine how much credit value climate-smart agricultural practices unlock under 45Z.
MARKET OUTLOOK
The U.S. market should continue to add SAF capacity through 2026–2028 as already-financed projects come online, including Montana Renewables' expansion and further Diamond Green Diesel debottlenecking. However, the policy environment is less straightforward than headline capacity figures suggest. The 2029 sunset, lower SAF credit value, pending 45ZCF-GREET/FD-CIC finalisation, and North American feedstock requirement all create uncertainty for project economics. The main shifts to watch are: greater use of domestic, Canadian and Mexican feedstock; stronger reliance on state-level incentives such as California and Washington's LCFS-style programmes; and lower attractiveness of Asian feedstock for U.S.-bound supply chains.
SINGAPORE OPPORTUNITY
Trading/structuring: help finance and structure Canadian and Mexican feedstock flows into U.S. 45Z-eligible supply chains, since these now carry a policy-driven premium over non-North American material.
Certification and traceability: Singapore-based sustainability certification and blockchain-traceability expertise (relevant given Acelen's Finboot blockchain partnership in Brazil, discussed below) is transferable to U.S. producers needing to prove North American feedstock origin under 45Z's new rules.
Book-and-claim intermediation: Singapore-based corporates and financial institutions can participate in book-and-claim SAF certificate purchases (the Google/American Airlines model) as part of their own Scope 3 decarbonisation, building commercial relationships with U.S. producers without needing physical Singapore-U.S. fuel logistics.
 
 
 
 
Canada
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
Canada's federal Clean Fuel Regulations (CFR) (in force since 2023 — require liquid fossil fuel suppliers to reduce the carbon intensity of the fuels they supply through a tradable-credit system, but the CFR does not itself mandate SAF use; SAF is simply one eligible low-carbon fuel among many that can generate credits. Credit-generating fuel mix in 2024 was dominated by ethanol (61.3%) and renewable diesel (27.9%), with SAF contributing just 0.8% — evidence that the CFR alone is not yet moving material SAF volume.
British Columbia's Low Carbon Fuel Standard (BC-LCFS) became the first jurisdiction in North America to mandate SAF blending, requiring 1% SAF in jet fuel by 2028, rising to 2% (2029) and 3% (2030), alongside jet fuel carbon-intensity reduction targets of 2% (2026) rising to 10% (2030). Vancouver International Airport separately offers incentive payments of CA$0.75–1.20 per litre for low-carbon jet fuel use.
In response to U.S. policy shifts eroding demand for Canadian-produced biofuel, the federal government introduced a time-limited Biofuels Production Incentive (over CA$370 million over two years, plus a separate CA$1.5 billion Clean Fuels Fund) for domestic biodiesel and renewable diesel producers from January 2026, and British Columbia introduced Canadian-content requirements effective January 1, 2026 requiring the province's 5% renewable-fuel minimum to be met with Canadian-produced material.
FEEDSTOCK
Canada's domestic feedstock base (canola oil, tallow, used cooking oil) is insufficient for its own renewable fuel ambitions, and producers increasingly import — most notably Braya Renewable Fuels in Newfoundland, which sources soy oil from Argentina (757,000 tonnes in 2024, up sharply from 48,000 tonnes in 2023).
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
Braya Renewable Fuels (the former Come By Chance refinery, Newfoundland) began renewable diesel production in February 2024 with 824 million litres/year capacity, and has stated plans to add SAF production and explore green hydrogen; 100% of current output is exported to the United States, since it is not logistically feasible to serve high-consumption Canadian regions from Newfoundland.
Tidewater Renewables (Prince George, BC) began commercial renewable diesel production in November 2023 (170 million litres/yr), and has publicly welcomed BC's Canadian-content rules as “levelling the playing field with imported biofuels.”
Parkland Corporation (Burnaby, BC) produced Canada's first batch of low-carbon jet fuel via co-processing in late 2024, and is working with industry to raise the ASTM D1655 limit on approved renewable co-processing feedstock from 5% toward 30%.
Imperial Oil is in advanced stages of bringing online a roughly 1-billion-litre renewable diesel facility near its Strathcona refinery in Edmonton, Alberta — one of several new capital projects (alongside Covenant Energy in Saskatchewan and Varennes Carbon Recycling in Quebec) reshaping Canadian low-carbon fuel supply.
R&D LANDSCAPE
Canadian SAF-relevant R&D centres on Natural Resources Canada's Clean Fuels Fund (de-risking hydrogen, renewable diesel, renewable natural gas, cellulosic ethanol, synthetic fuels and SAF projects), provincial low-carbon fuel credit-market design research, and applied engineering work on co-processing feedstock ratios (Parkland's ASTM pathway work).
MARKET OUTLOOK
Canada's near-term SAF outlook is bifurcated: British Columbia will generate  mandated SAF demand from 2028, while the rest of the country relies on the CFR's general credit market, which has so far generated negligible SAF volume. Export orientation (Braya to the U.S.) makes Canadian producers exposed to the same 45Z North American-content dynamics affecting U.S. domestic producers,  a potential tailwind if Canadian feedstock displaces disqualified Asian material, but a headwind if U.S. policy or trade friction disrupts cross-border flows.

Brazil
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
Brazil's “Fuel of the Future” Law (Lei No. 14.993/2024, enacted October 9, 2024) is the most comprehensive biofuels legislative package in the Americas, and includes the National Sustainable Aviation Fuel Programme (ProBioQAV), which — unlike blend-volume mandates elsewhere — sets a well-to-wake emissions-reduction target for domestic flights: 1% by 2027, rising 1 percentage point per year to 10% by 2037.
ProBioQAV sits alongside two other pillars of the law: the National Green Diesel Programme (PNDV), which raised the mandatory biodiesel blend from B14 (2025) to B15 (2026) en route to B20 by 2030; and an ethanol-gasoline blending ceiling raised to 30% (E30), up from 27%, with Brazil's National Energy Policy Council (CNPE) empowered to set the actual annual rate within that range. These sit on top of the pre-existing RenovaBio carbon-credit programme (2019), which requires fuel distributors to retire CBIO credits against a national decarbonisation target — 48.09 Mt CO₂ in 2026, rising to 72.54 Mt CO₂e by 2034 versus a 2018 baseline.
Brazil's National Agency for Petroleum, Natural Gas and Biofuels (ANP) is expected to publish detailed SAF production and marketing regulations in the second half of 2026 — the remaining piece of regulatory clarity the market is waiting on before further final investment decisions.
FEEDSTOCK
Brazil's feedstock advantage is unmatched in the Americas: soybean oil at industrial scale, an established sugarcane-ethanol base suited to Alcohol-to-Jet (ATJ) conversion (an estimated 2 billion litres of ethanol would be needed to meet ProBioQAV's 1.2-billion-litre 2037 SAF demand via ATJ alone), used cooking oil, and — distinctively — macaúba, a native, non-food oilseed palm being cultivated at industrial scale specifically for SAF feedstock.
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
Acelen Renewables (Mubadala Capital) — the standout Americas SAF project. Acelen secured $1.5 billion in financing in May 2026 (part of a total investment exceeding $3 billion) for a biorefinery in São Francisco do Conde, Bahia, targeting 1 billion litres/year of combined SAF and renewable diesel via HEFA technology from 2029, using soybean oil, used cooking oil and macaúba grown across 144,000 hectares of degraded land (20% earmarked for family-farmer partnerships). Financing was arranged by a 10-institution consortium led by HSBC and the IFC, including the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and Bank of China; roughly 90% of commercialisation volumes are already contracted, with named commercial partners including Trafigura, Moeve, Bunge and BGN, and the U.S. and Europe targeted as primary export markets.
A Fundação Getulio Vargas (FGV) study estimates the Acelen project's full value chain could generate up to $40 billion for the Brazilian economy and roughly 85,000 direct and indirect jobs over the next decade.
Petrobras (state oil major) plays a central role in Brazil's broader fuel-pricing and blending system and is expected to be a significant SAF offtaker/producer as ANP's 2026 regulations clarify the domestic market.
Traceability infrastructure: Acelen signed a 12-month partnership with Finboot in March 2026 to deploy a blockchain-based traceability platform (“Marco Track & Trace”) monitoring feedstock origin, farm-level output, emissions and sustainability compliance — a direct response to the credibility requirements of EU and U.S. SAF buyers.
 
 
R&D LANDSCAPE
Brazilian SAF R&D is anchored by RBQAV (the Brazilian SAF industry association), UNICA (sugarcane industry) research into ATJ pathway economics, and Insper's Agro in Data programme benchmarking HEFA and ATJ competitiveness. The University of São Paulo and Embrapa (Brazilian Agricultural Research Corporation) provide agronomic research relevant to macaúba cultivation and soy residue valorisation.
MARKET OUTLOOK
Brazil is, on the balance of the evidence gathered for this report, the clearest structural winner in the Americas SAF race over a 2027–2035 horizon: it combines (i) the region's largest and most diversified feedstock base, (ii) a legislated, if gradual, domestic demand signal from 2027, (iii) a marquee, substantially-financed flagship project (Acelen) that de-risks the investment case for follow-on capital, and (iv) explicit export orientation toward the two largest global SAF demand centres (EU, US). The principal risks are regulatory — ANP's SAF-specific rules are still pending — and execution risk on a first-of-scale integrated agro-industrial project with a 2029 start date.

Chile
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
Chile's relevant policy framework is its broader National Green Hydrogen Strategy, which leverages the country's exceptional wind resource in Magallanes (Patagonia) to position Chile as a green hydrogen and e-fuels exporter. Chile does not yet have a SAF-specific blending mandate; its policy relevance to this report is almost entirely through its role as the proving ground for Power-to-Liquid (PtL) technology that could eventually extend to e-SAF.
FEEDSTOCK
Chile's “feedstock” is fundamentally different from the biomass-based countries in this report: it is renewable electricity (wind) and captured CO₂ (currently biogenic/direct air capture), used to produce green hydrogen via electrolysis and then synthesise e-fuels — a pathway with no exposure to the used-cooking-oil or agricultural-feedstock competition affecting HEFA producers elsewhere in the region.
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
HIF Global — Haru Oni, Punta Arenas — the world's first fully integrated e-fuels plant, reaching FID in 2021 and producing its first litres of synthetic e-gasoline in December 2022. The demonstration plant uses a 3.4 MW wind turbine and 1.2 MW electrolyser to produce roughly 130,000 litres/year of e-gasoline in its initial phase, with partners including Porsche (anchor offtaker), Siemens Energy (technology), Enel Green Power, ENAP, GASCO and ExxonMobil. Planned scale-up targets were approximately 55 million litres/year by the mid-2020s and 550 million litres/year in subsequent phases, though the current focus is e-gasoline and e-methanol rather than e-SAF specifically.
HIF's broader ambitions have since moved capital toward other geographies — including a planned $4 billion e-fuels plant in Brazil (announced February 2026) and projects in the U.S. — suggesting Haru Oni's primary value to HIF now is as a proven technology demonstrator rather than the company's future volume centre.
R&D LANDSCAPE
Haru Oni functions as a live R&D platform for the full PtL value chain — electrolysis, direct air capture, and Fischer-Tropsch-adjacent synthesis — with Siemens Energy as lead technology partner. Chile's Ministry of Energy and state agencies (CORFO) have supported the project as a flagship of the national hydrogen strategy rather than through SAF-specific R&D funding.
MARKET OUTLOOK
Chile's realistic SAF outlook over the next five years is limited: e-SAF requires far higher capital intensity than HEFA (up to ~12x the cost of fossil jet, per IATA) and Haru Oni's own capital has been migrating to other geographies. Chile's genuine medium-term role is as a proof point and technology/engineering knowledge base for e-fuels more broadly, with any direct e-SAF volume unlikely before the early-to-mid 2030s absent a dedicated new FID.
SINGAPORE OPPORTUNITY[AW5] 
Colombia
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
Colombia's civil aviation authority, Aerocivil, issued the country's SAF Roadmap in January 2025 (Resolution 00090), developed jointly with Ecopetrol and the Colombian National Biofuels Producers Association (Fedebiocombustibles). The Roadmap targets 100 million gallons (378.5 million litres) of domestic SAF production by 2035 and 450 million gallons (1.7 billion litres) by 2050. A three-year cooperation agreement between Ecopetrol and Aerocivil, the “SAF Vuela” programme signed July 2025, supports the Roadmap through technical and regulatory coordination and environmental planning.
FEEDSTOCK
Colombia's feedstock base centres on palm oil (Colombia is a major global palm producer via Fedepalma) and its crop residues, used cooking oil, and — for the e-SAF pilot discussed below — green hydrogen. A January 2024 study specifically assessed SAF and renewable diesel production potential from oil palm and its crop residues in Colombia, and a joint MIT–Airbus–LATAM Airlines study found Colombia has one of the highest SAF production potentials in the LATAM region on the basis of its agricultural scale, cost competitiveness and existing biofuels expertise.
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
Ecopetrol (state oil major) began test production of SAF at its Cartagena refinery (Reficar) in late 2024 — an initial 20,000–32,000-barrel batch co-processed with up to 5% palm oil and used cooking oil, using technology developed by the Colombian Institute of Petroleum and Energies for the Transition (ICPET).
In April 2025, Ecopetrol and LATAM Airlines Colombia operated over 700 commercial flights using Jet A-1 co-processed with 1% renewable feedstock from Cartagena, distributed through Barranquilla, Medellín and San Andrés airports.
e-SAF pilot: Ecopetrol signed a strategic alliance with Germany's GIZ (German Agency for International Cooperation) in mid-2026 to conduct feasibility and engineering studies for a green hydrogen-based e-SAF pilot plant at Cartagena, targeting up to 800 tonnes/year, alongside Ecopetrol's existing 800-tonne/year green hydrogen “Coral Project” electrolyser — funded initially by Germany's Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action over a 24-month feasibility phase.
Ecopetrol has separately signalled plans to invest $500–700 million in a dedicated SAF production plant (potentially at Barrancabermeja), targeting operations by 2030, contingent on the Roadmap's regulatory framework.
R&D LANDSCAPE
Colombian SAF R&D runs through ICPET (Ecopetrol's internal research institute), academic PESTEL-framework policy analysis assessing SAF implementation barriers, and the GIZ partnership bringing German power-to-X engineering expertise to the Cartagena e-SAF feasibility study.
MARKET OUTLOOK
Colombia's trajectory is credible but slow: a published Roadmap with numeric targets, an engaged national oil company running real (if small) test batches, and now a second pathway (e-SAF) under feasibility study give Colombia more forward motion than Chile or Mexico. But volumes remain trial-scale through the late 2020s, with sustained commercial production not expected before 2028–2030 at the earliest, pending ANP-equivalent national regulation, feedstock certification frameworks, and the outcome of the GIZ feasibility study (due roughly 2028).
SINGAPORE OPPORTUNITY[AW6] 
Mexico
 
POLICY ENVIRONMENT
Mexico has no binding SAF mandate or dedicated biofuels law as of mid-2026. Pemex, the state oil monopoly, retains effective control over domestic fuel production, and has historically resisted policy changes perceived to threaten that position. A 2024 constitutional amendment reclassified Pemex and the Federal Electricity Commission (CFE) as “public enterprises,” and a 2025 reform package further entrenched CFE's role in energy generation and dispatch — reinforcing, rather than opening, state control of the energy system.
Analysts at Americas Market Intelligence assessed in 2024 that there was roughly a 50% probability of a biofuels law or mandate emerging under President Claudia Sheinbaum, citing her engineering background and her prior work (as Mexico City mayor) developing a used-cooking-oil-based biodiesel plant and a 2013 academic paper on Mexican UCO biodiesel potential — though resistance from Pemex and political pressure to preserve its monopoly remain the central constraint.
FEEDSTOCK
Mexico's own used-cooking-oil base is a net export commodity today rather than a domestic SAF input — mirroring the broader pattern of Latin American UCO flowing north into U.S. and (increasingly, per Section 2.2) European renewable fuel production rather than being processed domestically. A 2017 SENER (Ministry of Energy) technical roadmap and a subsequent Columbia SIPA capstone project (with the Mexican petroleum research institute and Pemex) both flagged feedstock availability, policy framework and financing as the “three F's” blocking a domestic Mexican SAF value chain — a diagnosis that remains largely current in 2026.
PRODUCTION CAPACITY AND KEY PLAYERS
Mexico is explicitly described by industry sources as not currently a producer of SAF, despite airline demand — a leading Mexican carrier reportedly used SAF only once, to ferry an aircraft from its U.S. manufacturing site to Mexico, citing cost (SAF running 2–3x conventional jet fuel) as the binding constraint. Two Latin American carriers — Chile's LATAM and Mexico's Aeroméxico — have publicly discussed alternative decarbonisation levers given persistent SAF scarcity in the region.
R&D LANDSCAPE
Mexican SAF-relevant research is limited relative to Brazil or Colombia, concentrated in the 2017 SENER technical roadmap (now dated) and academic capstone-style analysis (Columbia SIPA) rather than industrial-scale pilot projects. Separately, Mexico's Federal Electricity Commission (CFE) has explored a green-hydrogen partnership with Pemex at the Salina Cruz refinery — a potential long-run e-fuels angle, though not SAF-specific today.
MARKET OUTLOOK
Mexico is more relevant as a demand and feedstock-routing market than as a near-term producer. Its industrial centres, including Baja California Norte, Nuevo León and the Bajío region, could become important demand pools because of their concentration of export manufacturing and logistics activity. However, domestic SAF production is unlikely before the early 2030s unless there is a clear federal mandate, a change in Pemex's role, or a stronger policy push behind biofuels. For Singapore, the nearer-term opportunity is therefore linked to trade flows, UCO aggregation and nearshoring-related corporate demand rather than Mexican production capacity.
